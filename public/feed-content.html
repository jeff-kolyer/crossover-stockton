Overview

A problem whose scale is unknown

Dogs are being abandoned and found roaming Stockton's streets. Some are lost pets, some have been dumped, and others live with people experiencing homelessness. Their circumstances and needs differ.

Stockton Animal Services recorded 3,106 stray-dog intake events in 2024. In September 2026, Stockton Street Dogs reported encountering three to ten dumped dogs per week. These figures establish that dogs are entering the care system and being encountered by rescuers, but they do not tell us how many dogs currently need help or how many are going without care.

The scale of the unmet need remains unknown.

Care exists, but access is limited

The municipal shelter faces recurring capacity pressure: emergencies take priority, while healthy strays require appointments. Affordable veterinary care is in high demand, and rescue organizations depend on donations, volunteers, and available foster homes.

Related records: Animal Services describes regular capacity pressure and intake priorities · Reporting documented continued stray-dog pressure in Stockton · Animal Services describes a temporary found-animal foster pathway

A network of care providers

Stockton Animal Services handles municipal sheltering and animal-control calls; Stockton Street Dogs provides outreach, supplies, and rescue assistance; Hard Luck offers subsidized veterinary treatment; and St. Mary's Pathways provides transitional housing that accommodates pets. What each provider can offer at a given moment is not always clear.

Related records: Stockton Animal Services · Stockton Street Dogs · Hard Luck Animal Welfare Advocates · St. Mary's Community Services

Plans to expand care

Stockton is developing an animal shelter expansion costing roughly 10.47 million dollars and has authorized additional veterinary staffing and spay and neuter services. Stockton Street Dogs is proposing a Canine Campus for overflow and recovery. How much additional care these efforts will deliver remains to be established.

Related records: Stockton Animal Services · Stockton Street Dogs proposes a Canine Campus

Our work

Reviewing the field-call records

Crossover received 7,183 Stockton Animal Services field-call records on November 2 and began reviewing them November 4. We are tracing requests and recorded responses. A call is not a count of unique dogs or of the street population.
