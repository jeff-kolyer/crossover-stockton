Street dogs and access to care

Overview

November 12, 2026

What happens when a dog needs help in Stockton?

Dogs are being abandoned, shelters are under pressure, and people are trying to help. But how much help is actually reaching the dogs that need it?

In 2024, Stockton Animal Services recorded more than 3,100 stray-dog intakes across its service area. Stockton Street Dogs says it's now encountering three to ten dumped dogs a week.

Nobody knows how many dogs are currently on Stockton's streets.

And not every dog on the street is abandoned. Some are lost. Others have owners who may need help caring for them.

The city is expanding its shelter. Rescue groups are working to create more capacity. But space, veterinary care, and foster homes remain limited.

The question is whether help can reach the dogs that need it—and what's getting in the way.

Our work

We're beginning to find out.

We've visited Stockton Animal Services, applied to volunteer with two organizations, and started identifying places where dogs have been reported.

We also requested a year of animal-service calls from the city.

On November 2, we received 7,183 records.

Those records could tell us where people are asking for help, what they're reporting, and how the city responds.

We haven't finished analyzing them. And records alone won't tell us everything.

That's why we also need to get out into Stockton and meet the people doing this work.

What's next

From records to reality.

Our next stop is the St. Mary's and Church Street area, where shelters, food services, medical care, and transitional housing operate close together.

We want to see what's happening with dogs there, speak with people familiar with the area, and understand what help is available.

We'll also analyze the city's field-call records, follow up on volunteer opportunities, and track the expansion of shelter and veterinary care.

The goal isn't another report.

It's finding something useful we can actually do.
