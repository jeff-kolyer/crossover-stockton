# How AI Helps

Crossover does not use AI to replace human judgment.

AI helps Crossover pay attention.

Cities already produce signals: public notices, service hours, weather alerts, transit changes, outage reports, shelter updates, food access information, field observations, and community posts. The problem is that this information is scattered, stale, hard to compare, and often invisible to the people who need it most.

Crossover uses AI to gather those signals, organize them into structured records, and show where basic support may be thinning.

Humans verify what is real, correct what is wrong, coordinate with partners, and decide what to do.

## What AI does

AI helps collect fragmented civic information from public and partner sources.

AI turns that information into a small set of structured records: gaps, services, verified updates, signals, work items, and posts.

AI compares records against local conditions, such as time of day, weather, service availability, location, and known support needs.

AI flags likely gaps, changes, uncertainty, and places that may need human review.

AI helps keep the public feed updated so Crossover can see what is changing instead of relying on silence or stale directories.

## What AI does not do

AI does not run Crossover.

AI does not decide what is true.

AI does not replace human verification.

AI does not decide what should be done.

AI does not turn civic care into an automatic system.

It helps produce a clearer picture, but people remain responsible for judgment, care, and action.

## How the app works

The app renders structured civic records as a feed, a map, and a detail view.

Every visible item starts as a record.

A service record might show where food, water, hygiene, charging, shelter, or care is available.

A gap record might show where support appears to be missing, strained, closed, uncertain, or too far away.

A verified update might show that a human checked something and confirmed what is real.

A signal might show a public condition worth watching.

A work item might show what Crossover or a partner is doing in response.

The same record can appear as a feed tile, a map marker, and a detail view. The app does not invent a new page for every item. It renders the same civic record through a public spec.

## Why this matters

Most public support systems fail quietly.

A cooling center closes early. A charging station stops working. A shelter fills overnight. A food distribution changes hours. A public restroom is locked. A transit route stops reaching the people who depend on it.

These failures may be known by someone, somewhere, but they are rarely visible as a shared public picture.

Crossover uses AI to help turn scattered reality into public awareness.

The goal is not a perfect machine.

The goal is a better civic attention system than silence, stale directories, and disconnected institutions.

## The simple version

Crossover is not a website generated by AI.

Crossover is an organization with a public render spec.

AI gathers civic reality into structured records.

The app renders those records as a feed, map, and detail view.

Humans verify and respond.
