# Dogs without safe placement: public-source research baseline

**Review completed:** October 15, 2026  
**Research work:** `dogs-public-source-review`  
**Scope:** Dog welfare in Stockton, with shelter statistics labeled by their actual multi-jurisdiction coverage.

## What the evidence says

The strongest quantitative baseline available in this review is Stockton Animal Services' [2024 kennel report][sas-2024]. It records **3,786 dog intake events**, including **3,106 stray intakes**, and **3,742 dog outcome dispositions** during calendar 2024. These are flows through the shelter's service area, which includes Stockton and several other jurisdictions. They are neither a count of all dogs roaming Stockton nor a count of dogs currently awaiting care. The report also includes 76 spay/neuter-service intakes, so total intake is not identical to placement demand. [sas-2024]

The shelter's documented 2024 results include **1,003 dog adoptions**, **1,507 rescue transfers**, and **514 returns to owners**. Rescue transfer is a shelter outcome, not proof of a later adoption; adding rescue adoption totals to the shelter's transfer count would risk counting the same dog twice. The report also lists 514 euthanasia dispositions, 50 dogs that died, and 53 missing dispositions. It does not provide the beginning and ending dog population needed to reconcile intake with outcomes or measure current crowding. [sas-2024]

The existing [Stockton Street Dogs September 2026 record][ssd-record] reports a rise in dumped-dog encounters from roughly one per week a year earlier to about three to ten per week. That is an organizational field report, not a citywide census. The same dog could later appear in shelter or rescue figures; these counts must never be added together.

The [City's animal-shelter open-data page][dashboard] embeds a Power BI report, but both its embedded and direct views displayed **“This content isn't available”** when checked on October 15, 2026. This review also did not locate a downloadable 2025 or 2026 Stockton kennel report. Consequently, it cannot establish whether dog intake, outcomes, occupancy, or unmet placement demand rose or fell after 2024. The SSD report is a meaningful warning signal, but does not fill that statistical gap.

## What the existing shelter response accomplished

These are **dog outcome dispositions in the same 2024 service-area report**. The report's raw total includes additional disposition categories that its principal percentage chart excludes. Source checked October 15, 2026. [sas-2024]

| Outcome category | Dog dispositions |
| --- | ---: |
| Adopted | 1,003 |
| Rescue/transfer | 1,507 |
| Returned to owner | 514 |
| Euthanized | 514 |
| Died | 50 |
| Missing | 53 |
| Other: boarding 8, `RTOWSURR` 57, spay/neuter release 36 | 101 |
| **All reported dispositions** | **3,742** |

The 57 `RTOWSURR` dispositions are kept separate because the report gives them their own label; its meaning and relationship to the standard return-to-owner measure should be confirmed with Animal Services. Outcomes during 2024 need not involve dogs admitted during 2024. Without beginning and ending dogs in care, foster counts, and a consistent definition of usable kennel capacity, the difference between intake and outcome totals **cannot** be read as a change in shelter population. The report does not supply average dog length of stay or occupancy. [sas-2024]

### Veterinary, rescue, and housing results

The [Animal Balance agreement][animal-balance] targeted **approximately 200 pets of unspecified species across three clinic events**. That is contracted intent, not a count of completed surgeries. The [Hard Luck extension][hard-luck-extension] authorized four additional positions, but this review found no verified filled-post count, dog-specific procedure total, appointment throughput, or waiting-time series. The 2024 kennel report's spay/neuter intake and release categories are not a substitute for these measures. The [City's center acquisition record][center-acquisition] describes intended expanded veterinary and adoption services; it does not quantify treatment actually delivered.

The records establish that Stockton Street Dogs works at street and rescue level, but this review did not find a dated, complete, dog-level series of unique dogs assisted, rescued, fostered, treated, or adopted. Individual stories show work occurred but cannot establish program totals. Shelter transfers must be linked to receiving-rescue records before any systemwide result is calculated.

[Pathways Neighborhood's reported 268 beds][pathways-open] are **human transitional-housing beds**. They are not dog-inclusive placements. St. Mary's campus-wide pet-care references do not establish Pathways-specific admission rules, eligible dog capacity, or actual placements of people with dogs. Those quantities remain unknown.

## New findings relative to existing Crossover records

1. The [2024 City kennel report][sas-2024] adds an official dog-specific quantitative baseline that the existing qualitative gap records did not contain. It measures the shelter's broader service area, not Stockton alone. This is **new evidence in Crossover's research**, not proof of a new 2026 trend.
2. The [City's Animal Balance item][animal-balance] says **approximately 200 pets**, rather than “at least 200.” The existing evidence summary and project target were corrected; delivery remains unknown.
3. The current [open-data dashboard][dashboard] could not be read during this review. No numerical 2025–2026 trend or current occupancy claim has been added.

## Highest-value missing measurements

1. Monthly **2025–2026 dog intake and outcome counts**, with intake types, dispositions, and jurisdiction, from Animal Services' system or downloadable reports.
2. Beginning and ending **dogs in care**, including fosters; usable dog kennels, occupancy, and dog length of stay by month.
3. **Completed** dog and cat sterilizations and other treatments by program and date, plus appointment slots, waiting times, and filled veterinary positions.
4. Unique dogs served and final outcomes across SSD, shelter, fosters, and receiving rescues, with handoff IDs to prevent double-counting.
5. Pathways-specific pet admission rules, dog-inclusive places, and actual people-with-dog placements.
6. For each capacity project, the verified opening/implementation milestone and **net additional output** attributable to the project.

The corresponding machine-readable measurements, source references, unknowns, and project links are in [`dogs-research.json`](../../src/data/dogs-research.json). Future reviews should follow the [research refresh process](dogs-research-refresh.md).
