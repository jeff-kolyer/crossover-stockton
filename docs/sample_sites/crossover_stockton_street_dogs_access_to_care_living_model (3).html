Where Do We Start?

What needs fixing first—and what happens when we do

By ranking where the largest numbers of dogs fall out of the system, the records point to three immediate priorities. Toggle an intervention below to see how fixing one bottleneck relieves pressure across Stockton.

PRIORITY #1 Stops Intake at Source

Expand Spay/Neuter & Preventive Vet Access

Multi-month surgical waitlists and severe clinical shortages (such as limited vet staffing at Hard Luck Animal Welfare Advocates) lead directly to unplanned litters and preventable medical surrenders.

Active Effort Underway: Animal Balance Spay/Neuter Contract & High-Volume Clinics
PRIORITY #2 Closes Field Drop-Off

Fix Dispatch Triage & Appointment-Only Walls

When residents call among the 7,183 field logs or bring a found dog to 1575 S. Lincoln St, appointment-only barriers and dispatch delays leave strays roaming or dumped in corridors (3–10/week).

Active Effort Underway: Field-Call Latency Audit & Scan-Before-Shelter Triage
PRIORITY #3 Unblocks Kennel Exits

Expand Foster, Rescue & Pet-Inclusive Shelter Beds

3,106 stray intakes overwhelm existing kennels, while unhoused owners face scarce pet-friendly shelter beds (such as the 268 beds at St. Mary's Pathways).

Active Effort Underway: Spring 2027 Shelter Expansion & Proposed Canine Campus
