# Crossover Stockton

**See clearly. Act locally.**

Crossover Stockton is a civic investigation and evidence platform exploring how AI, robotics, automation, and human participation can help meet real-world needs in Stockton, California.

We begin with dogs on the street: investigating what happens to them, how they reach care, what prevents care from reaching them, and what could be done differently.

Our work consists of real actions, firsthand experiences, public records, conversations, experiments, and evidence-based findings. We publish what we learn, including what remains unknown.

**Live site:** https://crossoverstockton.org

## The crossover: From work to vocation

AI and robotics are becoming capable of doing more of the work around which we've organized human existence.

Crossover explores what people can do as these technologies free us from compulsory labor.

Instead of spending our lives working to earn the means to survive, we could devote more of our time and abilities to caring for one another, other living beings, and the world we inhabit.

That's the crossover: **from work to vocation.**

We begin by living that possibility now.

The work is not organized around producing something to sell. Its value is in what we discover, what we make possible, and what happens to the living beings we're trying to help.

Our approach brings together four things:

- **Technology:** Use emerging capabilities to remove obstacles, reduce administrative work, and make new forms of care possible.
- **Vocation:** Devote our time and abilities to work worth doing, not merely work that pays.
- **Investigation:** Discover what is happening through evidence, firsthand experience, and practical experiments.
- **Compassion:** Recognize and respond to the needs of other living beings.

We don't begin with a technological solution. We begin with reality.

## How Crossover works

**Discover actions. Make observations. Test pathways. Learn what happens.**

Crossover investigates real needs by participating in the world.

We visit places. We talk to people. We request records. We examine public claims. We test existing services and pathways to care. We attempt to help, document outcomes, and discover new possibilities.

Some actions produce knowledge. Some directly help someone. Some do both. Others reveal obstacles or contradict what we thought we knew.

There is no fixed list of work to complete. Each experience can reveal new questions, opportunities, and actions.

### Action → Activity → Findings → New Action

- **Action — What needs doing?** Identify questions to investigate, locations to check, people to contact, records to request, services to test, and opportunities to help.
- **Activity — What happened?** Record firsthand observations, conversations, requests, attempts, experiences, obstacles, and outcomes.
- **Findings — What did we learn?** Evaluate evidence, establish what is supported, distinguish facts from assumptions, and document what remains unknown.
- **New Action — What's next?** Turn questions, findings, and new opportunities into further investigations or practical attempts.

This is a continuous process, not a rigid sequence.

An observation may reveal a new question. A conversation may identify an unknown resource. A failed attempt may expose a problem worth solving. A finding may suggest several new actions.

Evidence can enter the investigation at any point.

**The work discovers the work.**

## Articles and accumulated knowledge

Our investigation articles are a central part of Crossover.

They bring together public records, reported conditions, firsthand activities, verified evidence, analysis, unanswered questions, and possible pathways forward.

An article isn't simply something we publish and move on from.

It's a living account of what we've learned about a problem.

As new evidence arrives, the articles can become more accurate, more complete, and more useful. Earlier conclusions can be challenged. Unanswered questions can become actions. Results from those actions can change the findings.

The articles are also tools for investigation.

We can share them with reporters, organizations, government agencies, researchers, and people already working on the problem. They provide context for conversations and give others an opportunity to correct, extend, or challenge our understanding.

What we discover becomes available for people and other AI systems to build upon.

**The knowledge is itself a resource. Its value grows through use, verification, and contribution.**

### Public gap pages

A public gap page summarizes the problem being investigated, what has been reported, what Crossover has established, and what remains uncertain.

It draws on the investigation's underlying evidence and findings.

As our understanding changes, the public account should change with it.

The purpose isn't to maintain a static explanation of a problem. It's to provide an increasingly reliable picture of reality.

## The investigation app

The investigation app organizes our work around four connected views:

**Tasks | Activity | Map | Findings**

The interface exists to help us understand and work with the underlying records. It is not the investigation itself.

### Tasks

Tasks describe Crossover's planned and pending work.

They may involve verifying reports, requesting information, contacting organizations, testing pathways, or following up on questions raised by findings.

Tasks can be Open, Waiting, or Completed.

A completed task means a documented step was completed. It does not mean the underlying problem was solved.

**Tasks are Crossover's actions, not a directory of volunteer opportunities or instructions for visitors.**

### Activity

Activity records what actually happened.

It includes field observations, conversations, records requests, volunteering, attempts to help, pathway tests, and their outcomes.

Activities may originate from existing tasks or be recorded independently.

An unsuccessful attempt is still an activity. A field visit where no dogs were observed is still evidence, provided the time, place, and circumstances are recorded.

Activity preserves the experience, not an invented conclusion about what it means.

### Map

The map connects investigation records to geographic locations.

It can show places identified in public reports, locations we're investigating, official organizations, and documented firsthand observations.

Tasks, activities, and other records can reference the same location without creating duplicate underlying locations.

The map changes with the selected investigation view:

- **Tasks:** Locations associated with work in the selected status filter.
- **Activity:** Locations associated with recorded activities.
- **Map:** Investigation locations, filtered by Recent, Historical, Official, or Firsthand.
- **Findings:** Evidence-based understanding and questions that can guide further geographic investigation.

Marker colors distinguish different kinds of evidence or sources:

- **Orange — Recent:** Recent public reports.
- **Gray — Historical:** Older public reports.
- **Blue — Official:** Government-related locations and sources.
- **Green — Firsthand:** Documented Crossover firsthand evidence.

Marker icons indicate what a location or record represents. A location can have multiple associated records with different dates, sources, and verification states.

Planned visits must never be represented as completed fieldwork. Public sightings must not be presented as current verified conditions.

Sensitive locations may be generalized or withheld.

The shared map helps connect what we're learning to actual places, and helps us decide where to investigate next.

### Findings

Findings organize what the evidence tells us.

They distinguish established facts, supported interpretations, unresolved questions, contradictory reports, and opportunities for further investigation.

Findings are supported by evidence and remain open to revision.

They are not merely conclusions at the end of an investigation. **The questions within findings are a source of new actions.**

A finding may lead us to request a record, contact someone, revisit a location, compare two organizations' procedures, or test a route to care.

The resulting activity contributes new evidence, which may strengthen, change, or contradict the original finding.

**Activity records what happened. Findings examines what it means.**

## Testing pathways to care

Crossover does more than collect information.

We investigate whether existing pathways actually connect people, resources, and technology to unmet needs.

For the street-dog investigation, possible pathways include:

- Reporting a dog and following the response.
- Applying to volunteer at an animal-care organization.
- Participating in a shelter's approved dog-outing program.
- Testing how supplies reach caregivers and animals.
- Investigating foster placement and adoption procedures.
- Establishing routes to veterinary treatment.
- Testing approved transportation assistance.
- Following an animal's case to a documented outcome.
- Learning from people already providing care.
- Testing whether AI can reduce the administrative work involved in helping.

These are possible investigations, not claims that the pathways work or that Crossover has completed them.

The important questions are practical:

What does someone have to do? What happens when they try? Where does the process break down? What do experienced caregivers actually need? Could technology remove an obstacle?

We may discover pathways we haven't imagined.

**The purpose is not to prove that technology is the answer. It's to discover where technology and human participation can genuinely improve what happens.**

## Working with AI

AI helps Crossover organize, examine, connect, and act upon evidence.

Someone might report:

> I visited Louis Park this morning, looked around for twenty minutes, and didn't see any dogs.

AI could help turn that conversation into a structured activity record, requesting only essential missing details.

From the accumulated activity and evidence, AI can help:

- Identify connections among reports, locations, people, organizations, and events.
- Update existing findings and articles with new evidence.
- Identify unanswered questions and contradictory information.
- Propose actions based on what remains unknown.
- Track requests, responses, and unresolved follow-ups.
- Analyze public records and compare claims with documented outcomes.
- Prepare communications and investigation summaries.
- Identify repetitive administrative work that could be simplified or automated.

The goal is to let people spend more of their time investigating, caring, and participating in the world, while AI handles more of the work of organizing information.

The website, conversational AI, and future integrations should operate from the same underlying evidence and records.

This is the intended direction of the system, not a claim that every AI-assisted workflow or integration is already implemented. Automated submissions and publication require appropriate implementation, authorization, and review.

AI must not invent observations, silently change evidence, or publish unsupported claims as established findings.

**People experience reality. AI helps us learn from those experiences and decide what to investigate next.**

## Evidence principles

Our public knowledge must remain accountable to its sources.

- **Plans aren't activities.** Intentions do not establish that something happened.
- **Attempts aren't outcomes.** Trying to help does not prove the attempt succeeded.
- **Reports aren't verified observations.** Preserve their sources, dates, and limitations.
- **No observation isn't proof of absence.** Record what was checked, where, when, and for how long.
- **Questions aren't conclusions.** An unresolved possibility remains an open question.
- **Contradictions matter.** New evidence may challenge previously accepted findings.
- **Findings can change.** Preserve the reasoning and evidence behind meaningful revisions.
- **Protect vulnerable people and animals.** Avoid exposing unnecessary identifying information or precise sensitive locations.
- **AI is not a witness.** It can analyze and organize evidence but must not fabricate firsthand experience.

An honest record of uncertainty is more valuable than a convincing but unsupported answer.

## Current investigation

### Dogs on the street in Stockton, California

We're investigating what happens to dogs living or roaming on Stockton's streets, what care is available, and what prevents that care from reaching them.

Public records and reports establish that dogs are entering animal services and being reported in the community. They do not by themselves establish how many dogs currently need care on the streets, where those dogs are, or what happens to every animal.

Our work includes checking reported locations, pursuing public records, contacting organizations, testing care pathways, and documenting firsthand experiences.

As the investigation develops, we aim to understand not only the animals' circumstances but the work already being done by people who help them.

What takes their time? What limits their efforts? What resources are missing? What could AI, robotics, automation, or additional human participation make possible?

Crossover has also identified other needs in Stockton. The same method can be applied to additional investigations as our work expands.

## Technology

The current platform uses:

- React 19, TypeScript, and Vite
- Leaflet and MapLibre mapping
- Static JSON records and Git history
- Build-time validation and prerendering
- Cloudflare Pages hosting

The public site currently operates without a database.

The map and interface provide views of the investigation records. The underlying evidence, relationships, and history matter more than any particular interface.

AI-assisted updates, conversational record management, and external system integrations require additional implementation and authorization.

The architecture should support a future in which people and AI systems can use the same structured, evidence-based information without depending on the public website's interface.
