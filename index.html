Street dogs and access to care
Updated November 11, 2026

Overview

Dogs are being abandoned and found roaming Stockton's streets. An established care network responds, but capacity and access remain significant challenges. The number currently needing help is unknown.

Sources and related work
CBS Sacramento reports abandoned-dog concerns around Louis Park, Stanislaus Street, and Highway 4 · No verified current count of dogs on Stockton's streets has been established. · Stockton Animal Services

The scale remains unknown

Stockton Animal Services recorded 3,106 stray-dog intake events in 2024. Stockton Street Dogs reported encountering 3–10 dumped dogs per week in September 2026. These figures measure different things. No verified count exists of dogs currently living or roaming on Stockton's streets.

Sources and related work
3,106 dog intake events · Stockton Street Dogs reported encountering 3–10 dumped dogs per week in September 2026. · No verified current count of dogs on Stockton's streets has been established.

Care exists, but access is limited

Stockton has an established animal-care network, but access to care is limited. The municipal shelter regularly faces capacity pressure, prioritizing emergencies while requiring appointments for healthy strays. Affordable veterinary care is in high demand, and rescue organizations depend on donations, volunteers, and available foster homes. Not every roaming dog is abandoned; some are lost or owned, including dogs living with people experiencing homelessness.

Sources and related work
Animal Services describes regular capacity pressure and intake priorities · Reporting documented continued stray-dog pressure in Stockton · Animal Services describes a temporary found-animal foster pathway · Animal Services distinguishes roaming dogs from abandoned dogs

A network of care providers

Several organizations help fill these needs. Stockton Animal Services handles municipal sheltering and animal-control calls; Stockton Street Dogs provides outreach, supplies, and rescue assistance; Hard Luck offers subsidized veterinary treatment; and St. Mary's Pathways provides transitional housing that accommodates pets. Each service has limits, and actual availability is not always clear.

Sources and related work
Stockton Animal Services · Stockton Street Dogs · Hard Luck Animal Welfare Advocates · St. Mary's Community Services

Plans to expand care

Efforts to expand care are underway. Stockton is developing a roughly $10.47 million animal shelter expansion and has authorized additional veterinary staffing and spay/neuter services. Stockton Street Dogs is proposing a Canine Campus for overflow and recovery. How much additional care these efforts will deliver remains to be established.

Sources and related work
Stockton Animal Services · Stockton Street Dogs proposes a Canine Campus

Our work

Reviewing the field-call records

Crossover received 7,183 Stockton Animal Services field-call records on November 2 and began reviewing them November 4. The review is in progress. We are examining what happens when someone asks for help, while documenting the records' coverage and limitations. Calls do not establish a count of unique dogs or the street population.

Sources and related work
Received Stockton Animal Services field-call records · Review Animal Services field-call records

Firsthand work and outreach

Crossover visited Stockton Animal Services and submitted a volunteer application. We also asked about Doggie Day Out and applied to volunteer with Stockton Street Dogs. Participation and onboarding have not yet been established by those applications.
