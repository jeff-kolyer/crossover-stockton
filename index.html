Street dogs and access to care
Updated November 11, 2026

Overview

The scale remains unknown

Dogs are being abandoned and found roaming Stockton's streets. Stockton Animal Services recorded 3,106 stray-dog intake events in 2024; Stockton Street Dogs reported seeing three to ten dumped dogs per week in September 2026. These figures measure different things, and no verified count exists of dogs currently living or roaming on the streets. Some roaming dogs are lost or owned, including dogs living with people experiencing homelessness.

Sources and related work
CBS Sacramento reports abandoned-dog concerns around Louis Park, Stanislaus Street, and Highway 4 · 3,106 dog intake events · Stockton Street Dogs reported encountering 3–10 dumped dogs per week in September 2026. · No verified current count of dogs on Stockton's streets has been established. · Animal Services distinguishes roaming dogs from abandoned dogs

Care exists, but access is limited

The municipal shelter faces recurring capacity pressure: emergencies take priority, while healthy strays require appointments. Affordable veterinary care is in high demand, and rescue organizations depend on donations, volunteers, and available foster homes.

Sources and related work
Animal Services describes regular capacity pressure and intake priorities · Reporting documented continued stray-dog pressure in Stockton · Animal Services describes a temporary found-animal foster pathway
Updated November 11, 2026

A network of care providers

Stockton Animal Services handles municipal sheltering and animal-control calls; Stockton Street Dogs provides outreach, supplies, and rescue assistance; Hard Luck offers subsidized veterinary treatment; and St. Mary's Pathways provides transitional housing that accommodates pets. What each provider can offer at a given moment is not always clear.

Sources and related work
Stockton Animal Services · Stockton Street Dogs · Hard Luck Animal Welfare Advocates · St. Mary's Community Services
Updated November 11, 2026

Plans to expand care

Stockton is developing an animal shelter expansion costing roughly 10.47 million dollars and has authorized additional veterinary staffing and spay and neuter services. Stockton Street Dogs is proposing a Canine Campus for overflow and recovery. How much additional care these efforts will deliver remains to be established.

Sources and related work
Stockton Animal Services · Stockton Street Dogs proposes a Canine Campus

Our work

Reviewing the field-call records

Crossover received 7,183 Stockton Animal Services field-call records on November 2 and began reviewing them November 4. We are tracing requests and recorded responses. A call is not a count of unique dogs or of the street population.

Firsthand work and outreach

Crossover visited Stockton Animal Services and applied to volunteer there. We also asked about Doggie Day Out and applied to volunteer with Stockton Street Dogs. Neither application establishes participation or completed onboarding.
